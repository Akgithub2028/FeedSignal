# FeedSignal optional founder validation runbook

**Scope update, 6 October 2026:** interviews were waived for M0. This optional future runbook and its blank worksheets are retained for reference; they are not outstanding M0 tasks or completed evidence. The current scope and pricing research are in [STATUS.md](STATUS.md) and [RESEARCH_REPORT.md](RESEARCH_REPORT.md).

Purpose: establish whether 2–10-person B2B SaaS teams repeatedly lose useful product evidence across existing customer channels and will pay for the proposed workflow. Target at least 15 qualified interviews. YC is a prospect filter, not proof of need or affiliation.

## Qualification and recruitment

Seek a founder or person who both reviews customer feedback and influences purchases. Qualify on a current paying customer base, two or more feedback channels or a demonstrably costly single channel, and a recurring product-prioritization decision. Record actual monthly feedback volume and time spent; the proposed 50–1,000 conversations/month range is a hypothesis, not an exclusion justified by evidence yet.

Avoid counting pre-launch projects with no customers, enterprise procurement teams, or founders merely praising AI. Keep disqualified interviews as learning, but do not count them toward the 15 qualified interviews. Interview through existing relationships or channels the owner authorizes; this document does not authorize an agent to send outreach.

Recruit in two waves: eight initial interviews, inspect themes, then seven interviews selected to challenge those themes. Include spreadsheet users, Linear-centric teams, and teams already paying for feedback tools. Do not select only friends or enthusiastic AI users. Consent separately to conversation notes, feedback examples, and evaluation reuse.

## Thirty-minute interview

1. **Five minutes: context.** What product, team size, customer count, buyer role, feedback channels, and current tools? Who makes roadmap decisions?
2. **Ten minutes: the last real decision.** Ask them to show how they chose a recent feature, bug fix, or deliberate rejection. Where did evidence come from? What was missed or duplicated? How was customer identity verified? What changed afterward?
3. **Five minutes: cost and failure.** How often does this occur? Ask for last week's time spent, interruption cost, examples of mistakes, and existing subscription spend. Separate recollection from measured records.
4. **Five minutes: artifact review.** Show a draft brief built from their permissioned data. Ask which claim is wrong, irrelevant, missing context, or unsafe. Compare it directly to their current workflow. Ask what decision it supports; an attractive summary alone is insufficient.
5. **Five minutes: commitment.** Present one concrete $59 or $99 monthly offer with scope. Ask whether they would keep using it, who approves payment, and what would prevent purchase. Request a dated next step, not a compliment.

Do not lead with feature suggestions or ask hypothetical "Would you use AI?" questions. If a team has no recurring problem, record that. Never imply calibrated churn prediction or guaranteed revenue recovery; those claims are not established by the baseline.

## Synthesis and next-step decision

For each pain, report the number of **distinct qualified teams** observing it, a recent example, its recurring cost, disagreement/counterexamples, the cheapest existing workaround, and the smallest proposed improvement. Count a team once even if they mention the problem repeatedly. Do not equate frequency with severity or convert reported pipeline value into realized revenue.

Rank the top three problems by observed recurring consequence and willingness to change/pay. Separate connector requests that block activation from new feature wishes. Preserve all current connector implementations; do not build additional connectors solely from a hypothetical request.

Use these commitment levels: verbal interest, agreed follow-up, continued-use pilot commitment, explicit price commitment, and payment. Report them separately. A commitment requires a named/pseudonymous team, decision maker, date, scope, and next action recorded with permission. A payment must be evidenced by an actual transaction; never invent one.

M2 commercial gate: three qualified teams agree to continued use and at least two explicitly accept the proposed price. These may be captured asynchronously without interviews. If absent, revise segment/offer before expanding M3–M6. `UNANSWERED_PRIORITIZED_INTERVIEW_FINDINGS` is superseded for M0 by the public research report.
